# Taste profile

**IS**
- Exact: every figure is a sign reading at a fixed place; tabular, aligned, dot-matrix true. No decorative numbers.
- Wayfinding-plain: one road, two lanes, readable at a glance like a highway sign at 100 km/h. Big type, few words.
- Alive: traffic keeps moving while the run is live; LED digits switch; signals change state.

**NEVER**
- Fintech-slick: no gradients, glass cards or neon glows on everything.
- Cute: no cartoon cars, emoji, mascots.
- Noisy: no particle fields, no motion that hides a number.

**Audience taste:** developers and flag/experimentation engineers: they admire Linear, Vercel dashboards, Grafana; they trust confidence intervals and hashes shown in full. Global, English, read left to right.
**Brand anchors:** none exist (project is new). Flagsmith appears only as link text. Old colours: none.
**Feel in motion:** mechanical and steady. Traffic glides at constant speed (linear only for vehicles on the road, as marquees). UI elements snap like signal heads (120-200 ms, steep ease-out). LED digits change instantly with a row-scan (each row lights 18 ms after the one above). Reveals 600-900 ms expo-out. Rollout merge: 1.6 s.
**Material:** retroreflective sheeting (flat colour, crisp white borders with rounded corners), brushed aluminium, asphalt grain (very subtle), LED dots with visible unlit dots.
**Taste anchors:** US/EU motorway gantry signs (white-bordered green panels, Highway Gothic letterforms); express-lane dynamic toll signs (amber LED price per lane); lane-use control signals; work-zone orange boards; airport-style precision dashboards.
**Lines not to cross:** no real highway shields or agency marks, no real company logos (Flagsmith only as text link), no fake numbers outside replay, no glowing neon-on-black overload.
**Comfort:** viewed on desktop monitors and in a 1080p screen-recorded video; also phones. Light concrete ground by day for comfort and video legibility; the LED signs supply the dark contrast in small housings, not the whole page.
